## What changed in the rerun

The first A2 run sent `Summarize the following document.` without the document, so all summarization answers were non-answers. The rerun made these changes:

- All 7,998 summarization main answers and 119,970 extra samples were regenerated with the document in the prompt. QA, dialogue and TruthfulQA answers are unchanged.
- The refusal filter now also catches apologies that say the model can't answer, in every subset.
- The labeller now checks long documents in overlapping windows of 300 words, because NLI truncates at 512 tokens.
- A3, B1, B2, labels and B3 to B9 were rerun.

Dialogue and QA prompts still omit `context`, so those answers are closed-book. Reference comparison works for QA, but dialogue answers are judged against knowledge the model didn't see.

Summarization labels look too strict: 82.4% of answers are labelled hallucinated, and on average 76% of each summary's sentences count as unsupported. Sentence-level NLI is strict with paraphrases, and lines such as "Here is a summary of the given text:" count as sentences.

## 2. How the 24,652 answers were selected

Nothing was randomly sampled down. The 24,652 answers are the whole calibration split, with one answer per prompt ([src/data/freeze_dataset.py](../src/data/freeze_dataset.py), `data/split_manifest.json`).

1. Merge all of HaluEval (QA, dialogue, summarization) and TruthfulQA: 30,817 rows.
2. Remove rows with an exact duplicate normalized prompt/source key (`norm_key`): 2 rows, both summarization, leaving 30,815.
3. Flag 2 rows whose answer appears in both the correct and incorrect reference lists. They are kept, and both fall in calibration.
4. Split with `StratifiedGroupKFold`: 5 folds, seed 42, stratified by dataset + subset, grouped by `norm_key`. The first fold is test and the other four are calibration.
5. Result: 24,652 calibration rows (80.0%) and 6,163 test rows (20.0%), with 0 shared `uid` and 0 shared `norm_key` values.
6. Generate one answer per calibration prompt with Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct: temperature 0.7, top-p 0.9, `max_new_tokens` 128, float16. Dialogue, QA and TruthfulQA used seed 42 one answer at a time. Summarization was regenerated in batches of 256 with one seed per batch.
7. For labelled analyses, drop the 3,427 refusals, leaving 21,225.

The test split has no generated answers yet.

## 5. Answers that hit the 128-token limit

**14,058 of 24,652 main answers (57.0%).**

| Subset | Answers | At limit | % |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| Dialogue | 8,000 | 3,586 | 44.8 |
| QA | 8,000 | 4,004 | 50.0 |
| Summarization | 7,998 | 6,029 | 75.4 |
| TruthfulQA | 654 | 439 | 67.1 |
| **Total** | 24,652 | 14,058 | 57.0 |

- **This is an estimate.** A2 saved neither the stop reason nor the token IDs, so the count is answers with at least 128 tokens after re-tokenizing the saved text in A3.
- **Evidence:** 13,025 of the 14,058 (92.7%) don't end in sentence-final punctuation, against 256 of the other 10,594 (2.4%).
- **Uncertainty:** re-tokenizing can shift lengths slightly. 67 answers came out at 129 or 130 tokens, and 209 of the 353 answers at 127 tokens also lack final punctuation, so the true count may be a little higher.
- **Labelled answers:** 13,040 of 21,225 (61.4%) hit the limit. Of those, 85.0% are labelled hallucinated, against 89.2% of the rest.
- **Knock-on effect:** the limit caps output length, word count and every count-based feature. Truncated answers can also lose the part of the answer that would be judged.
- **Not measured:** the 369,780 extra samples, because the tokenizer isn't in this repository.

## 7. Hedging and repetition

**Decision:** keep the Chapter 4 plots and correct Chapter 3. Both features are already implemented in [src/extract_features/extract_linguistic_features.py](../src/extract_features/extract_linguistic_features.py), saved in `data/a3/linguistic_features.parquet`, and used by B7 and B8. "Future work" is out of date. Confirm that the plots use `hedge_rate` and `repetition_rate` from that file, and redraw them from the rerun data.

### Text for Chapter 3

> **Linguistic features.** We computed two linguistic features from the text of each generated answer. Answers were lowercased and split into word tokens with the regular expression `\b[\w'-]+\b`. Let $N$ be the number of word tokens in an answer.
>
> *Hedging rate.* We matched the answer against a fixed lexicon of 42 hedging phrases (for example, "as far as I know", "it seems", "may be") and 19 hedging words (for example, "perhaps", "likely", "roughly"). Phrases were matched first, longest first, and overlapping matches were counted once. With $h$ the number of matches, the hedging rate is
>
> $$\text{HedgeRate} = \frac{h}{N}$$
>
> *Repetition rate.* With $U$ the number of distinct word tokens in the answer, the repetition rate is the share of tokens that repeat an earlier token:
>
> $$\text{RepetitionRate} = \frac{N - U}{N} = 1 - \frac{U}{N}$$
>
> Both rates are 0 when $N = 0$. Both are heuristics: the hedging lexicon was hand-written and was not validated against human annotation, and the repetition rate counts function words and does not measure repeated phrases or ideas.

### Text for Chapter 4

> Hedging was rare: 81.1% of answers contained no hedge, and the mean hedging rate was 0.0027 (SD 0.0063). It carried no signal for the provisional hallucination label (r = -0.001, AUROC = 0.496). The mean repetition rate was 0.219 (SD 0.084). Pooled across subsets, repetition was lower in answers labelled hallucinated (0.229 against 0.257; r = -0.126), though mean token entropy was the strongest of the 16 single-pass features (r = 0.163). The pooled repetition result should be read with care. Repetition rate is correlated with answer length (r = 0.68), and its direction changes by subset: higher repetition went with hallucination in dialogue (AUROC 0.599), there was no effect in summarization (0.500), and lower repetition went with hallucination in QA (0.453) and TruthfulQA (0.424). Part of the pooled effect therefore reflects differences between subsets.

| Feature | r with label | AUROC: all | Dialogue | QA | Summarization | TruthfulQA | B8 GB importance | B8 LR coefficient |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Hedge rate | -0.001 | 0.496 | 0.518 | 0.528 | 0.488 | 0.462 | 0.004 | -0.104 |
| Repetition rate | -0.126 | 0.392 | 0.599 | 0.453 | 0.500 | 0.424 | 0.148 | -0.461 |

Repetition rate is B8 gradient boosting's second most important feature, but that importance comes from the pooled data, so don't present it as evidence that repetition causes or reveals hallucination.

### What stays as future work

- Validating the hedging lexicon against human annotation
- Measuring phrase-level or semantic repetition, and excluding stopwords
- Controlling for answer length and subset
